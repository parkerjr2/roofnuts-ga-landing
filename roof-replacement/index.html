When is roof replacement the appropriate next step?

Ask for documented findings rather than a recommendation based only on age. The contractor should explain the extent of the concerns, whether a repair was considered, and what a replacement is intended to resolve.

Your reasons for exploring replacement might include repeated problems, extensive documented damage, or planning an upgrade alongside other exterior work. Those situations justify a discussion; they do not establish the same scope for every home.

If you have one isolated concern and no assessment yet, start with a roof inspection. If a contractor believes a defined repair will address the issue, compare that proposal with the larger project using our repair guide.

Compare the whole project, not just the shingle name

Two proposals can specify the same shingle and include very different work. Ask each contractor to describe the existing roof removal, installation details, cleanup, and responsibilities in writing.

Choosing roofing materials for your home

Start with the roof you have, the appearance you want, and the conditions the contractor needs to address. Ask which options the contractor actually installs and why each is suitable for your project.

For asphalt shingles, compare the exact product and installation requirements rather than relying on broad labels such as “premium.” If you are considering metal or another roof covering, request a scope specific to that system; an asphalt-shingle estimate is not a complete basis for comparing it. Identify low-slope sections, porches, or additions so they are not overlooked in a quote focused on the main roof.

Ask for product samples and written warranty terms before selecting a color or package. HOA or historic-review requirements should be resolved before ordering materials when they apply. A product advertised for a performance characteristic should be evaluated using its actual documentation, not a general claim that it makes the home stormproof.

How much does a roof replacement cost in Georgia?

The price needs to reflect your roof, not just your home’s floor area. GAF identifies roof size, material, pitch, complexity, and location among replacement cost factors. Its national cost information is not a quote for a Georgia property. See GAF’s roof-cost guide.

Ask for the measured roofing area and the assumptions behind the estimate. Then clarify what is included in the base price and what may change after removal begins. For example, a proposal can state how additional deck repairs will be documented, priced, and approved without pretending concealed conditions are already known.

Compare total scope, exclusions, payment schedule, and warranty responsibilities. If financing is offered by a contractor or lender, evaluate its separate terms; this referral service does not promise financing availability or approval.

Permits, Georgia codes, and local approval

Georgia’s current mandatory codes include the 2024 International Residential Code with Georgia amendments. The state publishes its current code list through DCA. Ask the contractor and the local authority to identify the rules applicable to your property and planned work.

Before work begins, clarify who will confirm whether a permit is required, who will obtain it, and who will schedule any required inspections. If the home has known historic or HOA restrictions, include those in the planning conversation. Do not assume that choosing a similar color settles every approval question.

Our city guides provide local research starting points for Alpharetta, Newnan, Warner Robins, and Brunswick. For another location, use the Georgia city directory. Local requirements should be reconfirmed for the actual project.

What the replacement process should clarify

  1. Assessment and measurement: Understand the findings and the proposed roof area.
  2. Written scope and selections: Confirm materials, included work, exclusions, and change procedures.
  3. Approvals and scheduling: Resolve applicable permissions, delivery, access, and weather contingencies.
  4. Installation and documentation: Ask how the contractor will report unexpected conditions and document authorized changes.
  5. Completion and records: Receive the agreed project documentation, warranty information, and contact process for questions.

Ask for a realistic schedule after the contractor knows the scope. Weather, complexity, material availability, and additional work can affect timing. Do not make a promised one-day installation the main basis for selecting a contractor.

Storm-related replacement and paying directly

A replacement estimate can be useful whether you are budgeting out of pocket or evaluating a possible insurance claim. Keep the construction proposal and the coverage discussion clear: the contractor defines proposed work, while your insurer evaluates coverage under your policy.

If you intend to make a claim, ask the insurer about documentation, deadlines, and your responsibilities. Review Georgia’s home insurance resources for a starting point. We do not promise approval, a waived deductible, or a free roof.

Understand who stands behind the work

Ask for the product warranty and any contractor workmanship warranty separately. Identify who handles each type of concern, what conditions apply, and whether registration or other steps are your responsibility.

Have the contractor explain any exclusions and the process for requesting service. Keep the signed scope, change orders, final invoice, product details, and warranty documents together. A referral from this website does not create a warranty from the website operator.

Roof replacement questions

Can I get an estimate before deciding whether to file a claim?

You can request an assessment and proposed scope while you are considering your options. Confirm any fee with the contractor, and ask your insurer directly about claim requirements and timing.

Does every old roof need replacement?

No age alone establishes what your roof needs. Ask for evidence of its condition and an explanation of reasonable alternatives. If recommendations conflict, an additional assessment may help you understand why.

Is removal of the existing roof included?

Check the proposal. Do not assume that “new roof” specifies removal, disposal, concealed-condition work, and all installation details. Ask the contractor to explain the proposed approach and confirm that it is appropriate and permitted.

Can the final price change after work begins?

A contract may provide for additional work when concealed conditions are found. Before signing, require a clear process for documentation, pricing, and authorization. Avoid leaving that conversation until the roof is open.

Will I need to leave the house?

Ask about noise, access, debris control, pets, parking, and any circumstances that affect occupancy. The contractor should explain the plan for your home rather than offer a blanket answer for every project.

What should I keep after the job is finished?

Retain the agreement, approved changes, payment records, product identification, warranty documents, and applicable permit or inspection records. Ask for the contractor’s recommended maintenance information and future service contact.
